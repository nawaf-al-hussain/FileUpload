The student shouldn’t have to guess what’s next.

01 / 08 · introduction
The problem

Content is not the same thing as knowing what a learner needs next.

Most study workflows can tell a student what a topic says. They struggle to maintain a governed picture of what the learner has demonstrated, where the gap is, and which intervention should follow.

02 / 08 · problem statement
The solution

Build the learning system around the learner’s relationship with the curriculum.

Curriculum-grounded

Curriculum and SpecificationPoints remain authoritative. Retrieval and language models are subordinate evidence mechanisms.

Evidence-driven

Attempts, marks and extracted interaction evidence become provenance-bearing learning signals rather than informal chat memory.

Learner-aware

A governed learner model turns evidence into mastery, misconceptions, diagnosis and next-best-action recommendations.

06 / 08 · product
Trust boundary
Embeddings ≠ educational truth.

Semantic similarity can retrieve candidates. It does not define what the curriculum means.

Chat ≠ mastery.

A learner utterance is evidence to interpret, not permission to rewrite governed mastery.

LLM ≠ marker.

Smart Mark remains validation- and agreement-gated; generated output is not automatically authoritative.

Retrieval ≠ automatically promoted.

Benchmarks decide whether a serving path is acceptable for educational use.

AI is powerful.
It is not authoritative.
07 / 08 · trust
The point

Don’t make the learner search for the next step.

SyllabAI is designed to make the next action a consequence of curriculum truth, learning evidence and diagnosis.
